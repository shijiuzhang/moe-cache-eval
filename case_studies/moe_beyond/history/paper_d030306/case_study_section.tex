\subsection{External case study: a third-party trace through the package}
\label{sec:case-study}

\paragraph{Why this case.}
MoE-Beyond~\cite{gavhane2025moebeyond} is the only study in our audit
(Appendix~A) that publishes both its traces and its trace simulator. It reports
that, for DeepSeek-V2-Lite at batch size one with 10\% of experts resident, a
learned predictor raises the cache hit rate from \CSreportedInfinity{} for the
MoE-Infinity heuristic to \CSreportedBeyond{}. Setting that comparison beside
other studies requires fields its public text does not state: event replay,
cache scope and capacity, hit counting, and workload construction. This section applies the released package to the public
artifact (commit \texttt{\CSthirdPartyCommit}) to recover those fields. It does
not re-evaluate the reported result. The analysis was pre-registered before any
result was computed; every deviation is listed with the artifact.

\paragraph{Execution contract.}
Running the published artifact at the reported configuration reproduced the
reported value (\CSreproInfinity{} vs \CSreportedInfinity{}). Running the
published artifact at the reported configuration reproduced the reported value
(\CSreproBeyond{} vs \CSreportedBeyond{}). Both are means over \CSseeds{} seeds of the
random cache pre-fill (tolerance $\pm$\CSreproTolerance{} percentage points).
On the in-event
eviction probe, the published simulator recorded \CSprobeAtwoMisses{} misses,
the count expected under per-access retention; its executed replay contract is
classified as per-access. On the reference trace of Section~2.5 the published
simulator recorded \CSprobeAoneHits{} hits and \CSprobeAoneMisses{} misses,
matching the event-atomic reference counts (12 / 18). Execution also confirmed
three contract fields: the cache is global and rebuilt, with a random pre-fill,
for every prompt; hits are counted per expert access; and prefetched experts
are not counted as hits but do change residency. The traces record one prefill
forward pass per prompt, so we declare two event definitions: per-token events,
matching the simulator's replay unit, and one whole-prefill event per layer,
matching the executed forward pass. At the reported operating point
(batch size one, 10\% residency, global scope, \CStheirCapacity{} blocks) under
either event definition, every event's expert set fits within capacity, so
in-event eviction cannot occur; whatever replay semantics the simulator uses, its
counts there coincide with the event-atomic contract. Axis~I does not operate in
this setting. At our per-layer frame with whole-prefill events, a fraction
\CSoverCapEtwoPerLayerTen{} of events exceed capacity; in that setting the replay
contract affects the counts and should be declared (checklist A1--A2).

\paragraph{Operating regime.}
Under our reporting frame, the per-token event stream of this setting has
$\bar r = \CSrbarEoneRhoForty$ at $\rho = 40\%$ (per-layer scope); the nearest
measured alignment point in Table~11 is the \CSnearestEone{}
($\bar r = \CSnearestEoneRbar$). Under our reporting frame, the whole-prefill
event stream of this setting has $\bar r = \CSrbarEtwoRhoForty$ at $\rho = 40\%$
(per-layer scope); the nearest measured alignment point in Table~11 is the
\CSnearestEtwo{} ($\bar r = \CSnearestEtwoRbar$).

\paragraph{Workload construction and residency headroom.}
The \CStestPrompts{} public test prompts have \CSheadsDistinct{} distinct
70-character heads, \CSfinalsDistinct{} distinct final lines and a 95th-percentile
word 5-gram Jaccard of \CSpromptJaccardPninetyfive{}; the collection scripts
apply no chat template and record no decode step. The public text describes
recording each token of the generated sequences, while the published collection
scripts run one prefill forward pass per prompt; we record the difference without
attributing it. On the public test prompts, the \S5.8 detector reports
flag~=~\CSdetectorFlag{}; this is a detector-level screening result, not a
replication or refutation of any workload effect in that study. The triggering
positional agreement sits at position~0, a shared beginning-of-sequence token
(\CSonlyBosShare{} of pairs agree only there), and at the question opener, in
sequences of \CSlenMin{}--\CSlenMax{} tokens; the threshold was validated on
512-token sequences. Excluding position~0 post hoc lowers the 95th percentile
from \CSposAgreePninetyfive{} to \CSposAgreeNoBosPninetyfive{}. This is a
limitation of our own detector: positional agreement is length-sensitive and
needs a length-aware threshold.
Under the package's prefetch-free contract (per-token events, per-layer scope,
$\rho = 40\%$), the offline optimum leaves a recoverable gap of
\CSgapRhoForty{}, of which the future-victim share is \CSfvShareRhoForty{}.
These are prefetch-free residency results under our replay contract and are not
comparable with the prefetch-inclusive hit rates reported by MoE-Beyond.
The case also bounds the decomposition of Section~7.2. With whole-prefill events
at $\rho = 40\%$ ($\bar r = \CSrbarEtwoRhoForty$), forced admission performs
worse than the best causal policy and the two shares leave $[0,1]$
(\CSadmShareBreak{} and \CSfvShareBreak{}): the decomposition presupposes
$m_B \le m_F \le m_c$, which no longer holds once most events exceed their quota.

\paragraph{What the package added.}
From the paper text alone, \CSfieldsPaperOnly{} of the \CSfieldsTotal{}
reporting-block fields can be filled; with the public artifact and the released
package, \CSfieldsWithArtifacts{} can be filled. The fields that only the package
made fillable are: retention decision, intra-event order, distinct prompt heads
and final lines, chat template, decode steps recorded, positional token
agreement, expert Jaccard on low-text-overlap pairs, cache scope, union per
layer-step, capacity per layer, $r(s,l)$, and the forced-admission decomposition.
Each is required before a hit rate measured under one contract can be set beside
one measured under another, and each was obtained by executing the published
artifact rather than by rereading the paper.
